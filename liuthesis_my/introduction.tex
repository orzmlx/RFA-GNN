\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Background}
\noindent Drug discovery is slow, expensive, and uncertain. Many candidate drugs fail before they reach the market~\cite{cbo2021rnd}. One reason is that drug effects are hard to predict. A drug may bind to one target protein, but the final response depends on many connected genes, proteins, and signaling pathways inside the cell~\cite{Brown_2025, AnnualReviews_2025}. Because these systems are highly connected, it is difficult to predict how a drug changes the state of a cell.

\noindent Recent work also shows a simple limitation of target based drug discovery. A drug may be designed to act on one main target, but its final effect can still depend on other molecules and on how the whole cellular system responds~\cite{sadri2023offtarget}. This thesis studies this broader prediction problem and the goal is to predict how gene expression changes the cell’s gene activity, compared to the same cell type without the drug.

\section{Drug perturbation}
Drug perturbation means using a drug to change the state of a cell so that the response can be studied. A drug usually starts by binding to one or more target proteins. This event may block a protein, activate it, or change its function in another way. The effect usually does not stop there. The signal can spread through interactions inside the cell and affect many downstream genes.

\noindent Usually, the cellular response is described through gene expression. Gene expression measures the quantity of RNA produced from each gene, a higher number means the gene is more active. After drug treatment, some genes become more highly expressed and others become less active. Predicting these changes gives a broad summary of the molecular response and can be measured directly.

\section{High-throughput experiment}
\noindent Researchers use high-throughput experiments to study drug perturbations at scale. The idea is simple. Many cell lines are grown in multi-well plates. Many drugs are applied under controlled conditions. Gene expression is measured after treatment. This design makes it possible to compare many perturbations in a standard way. Figure~\ref{fig:well_plates} shows this basic idea in a simple form.

\noindent Each plate includes two main types of wells:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Treatment wells}: These contain cells exposed to a specific drug, usually dissolved in DMSO~\cite{Santos_2003}.
    \item \textbf{Control wells}: These contain cells exposed only to DMSO, without the drug. They provide a reference for the same experimental setting without the drug effect.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The key comparison is the difference between a treatment well and a matched control well. This comparison helps separate the drug effect from the background state of the cell. The control must come from a similar treatment setting. For this reason, high-throughput experiments also record background variables such as cell line, drug dose, treatment time, and plate information, so treatment and control wells can be matched under comparable conditions. These variables matter because the same drug can act differently in different cell types and under different experimental settings.

\section{Prior knowledge of molecular interactions}

Another source of information comes from biological knowledge databases. These resources summarize known links among genes and proteins, such as control and signaling relations. This information indicates that drug effects do not come from one target alone, they may start from a small set of targets and its response is shaped by how signals move through the surrounding molecular network. One example of such a resource is OmniPath~\cite{turei2016omnipath}. Prior knowledge databases provide interaction structure that can be used to represent how signals may move between genes and proteins.


\section{Aim}
We focus on whether a graph based model can predict gene expression changes after drug treatment from the baseline state of the cell, known drug targets, and a molecular interaction network. Because more accurate response prediction can help researchers test compounds, choose which experiments to perform next, and understand drug action more clearly.

\noindent The aims of this thesis are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item To build a graph neural network (GNN) based on the molecular interaction network. This model predicts drug related gene expression changes by combining baseline cell data and drug target information.
    \item To test how well the model works on unseen drug targets and unseen cell lines where test drugs' targets or cells were never seen during training.
    \item To compare this model with baseline models that do not use a gene network.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related works}
Graph neural networks are now widely used in biology because many biological systems can be represented as graphs. A recent review summarizes GNN applications in single cell omics and discusses common architectures such as Graph Convolutional Network (GCN), GraphSAGE, and Graph Attention Network (GAT)~\cite{li2025gnn_sc_review, scarselli2009gnn, kipf2017semi, hamilton2017inductive, velickovic2018graph}. These models are useful because each node can update its representation by using both its own features and information from connected neighbours. In biology, this makes them a natural choice for pathways, control links, and other structured molecular systems.

\noindent More recent graph models also use Transformer style block designs. Graphormer shows that a standard Transformer architecture, combined with graph specific structural encodings, can work well for graph representation learning~\cite{ying2021graphormer}. GraphGPS extends this direction with a modular graph Transformer design that combines local message passing with global attention and reports strong performance across many graph tasks~\cite{rampasek2022graphgps}. These studies are important here because they support the broader idea that attention, FFN, residual connections, and normalization can also form useful repeated blocks in graph models.

\noindent Several studies have shown the value of graph based learning in perturbation biology. GEARS uses a gene relationship graph to predict responses to genetic perturbations in single cell data~\cite{roohani2023gears}. Cellograph models multi condition single cell datasets with graph based learning~\cite{shahir2024cellograph}. Evans et al. also study graph structured neural networks for perturbation biology more broadly~\cite{evans2024gsnn}. These studies focus on different data types and tasks, but they support the same basic idea that graph structure can help connect an intervention to downstream molecular changes.

\noindent Another line of work predicts drug effects without an explicit biological interaction graph. Large resources such as LINCS L1000 and Connectivity Map make this possible because they provide gene expression responses for many compounds and cell lines~\cite{l1000_paper}. DeepCOP~\cite{woo2020deepcop} is one example. It uses a multilayer perceptron and drug information to predict gene expression changes. This type of model is simple and scalable, and it provides a strong baseline. Still, it treats genes as separate outputs and does not use known molecular links among genes and proteins.

\noindent A different line of work uses known biology more directly. Regulation Flow Analysis models how drug effect signals may move through an interaction network collected from earlier studies and uses this structure to suggest possible mechanisms~\cite{roca2025rfa}. This kind of method is attractive because it is easier to explain biologically. Its limitation is that the signal moving rule is largely fixed by the network and does not learn directly from large drug response datasets. By contrast, data driven graph neural networks can learn how strongly information should move while still using the network structure.

\noindent Some recent studies combine graph structure with supervised prediction for drug response related tasks~\cite{wang2022graphgrp, kim2021gcn_drug_response}. These studies suggest that molecular networks can give a useful starting structure when labels are noisy or when the response follows many indirect molecular steps. They also show that graph quality matters. Resources such as OmniPath~\cite{turei2016omnipath} are useful because they provide directed interactions and sign information for signal flow.

\noindent Evaluation is also important in this area. Many studies report performance under random splits, where related drugs or cell contexts may appear in both training and test sets. For drug response prediction, this can make the model look stronger than it really is on unseen cases. A stricter setting tests unseen drugs or unseen cell lines. This thesis follows that view and studies whether a graph based model can work beyond the conditions seen during training.
