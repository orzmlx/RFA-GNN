\chapter{Results and Discussion}
This chapter reports the main results and further investigates the model behaviour. Specifically, it compares the predictive performance of different architectures, analyses how the scores change across different experimental settings, and interprets what the proposed model learns from the biological data.

\section{Evaluation framework}
\noindent All experiments in this chapter are conducted on the L1000 landmark setting with 978 genes. For every sample, the input consists of a matched control profile, drug related features, and cell context information. The target is defined as the response vector $y = x_{\mathrm{trt}} - x_{\mathrm{ctl}}$, which is consistently used across all evaluated models.

\noindent The evaluation framework compares three different models. The first is a DeepCOP style baseline, which concatenates the control profile and drug features into a standard feed-forward network. The second model is GSNN \cite{evans2024gsnn}, a graph-based baseline that utilizes message passing. The third model is our proposed method, referred to as Counterfactual Attention GNN, or CAGNN, throughout this chapter.

\noindent To rigorously assess model generalization, we use three distinct data split settings. The warm split tests unseen treatment and control pairs sampled from the same broad data distribution as the training set. The cold drug split holds out specific drugs from the training phase, while the cold cell split entirely removes test cell lines from training. The cold cell split is expected to be the most challenging setting, as a novel cell line not only changes the baseline expression state but can also fundamentally alter the cellular response to a familiar drug.

\noindent Beyond these standard splits, this thesis also introduces a training data budget evaluation. Rather than defining a new metric, this strategy systematically restricts the amount of available training data—capping the number of samples per cell-drug pair—while keeping the test set fixed. This approach helps to study data efficiency, clarifying how quickly each model's performance improves as more training instances become available.

\noindent The overall prediction quality is summarized using two main metrics. Mean squared error measures the numerical accuracy, where a lower value indicates that the prediction is closer to the observed response in absolute terms. Meanwhile, the sample-wise Pearson correlation coefficient evaluates whether the predicted response correctly captures the shape and trend of gene expression changes. Together, these two metrics provide a comprehensive view of model performance. The mean squared error focuses on the magnitude of the error, whereas the correlation coefficient focuses on the biological pattern of the response.

\section{Precision metrics}
The second group of metrics focuses on whether the models recover the most responsive genes. This view is important because perturbation analysis often cares more about the strongest up regulated and down regulated genes than about every small change in the full response vector. For this reason, top gene precision is treated as a separate part of the evaluation.

\noindent This analysis can be computed directly from the saved prediction files. In this chapter, the definition follows the XPert evaluation style. For each sample, the true response vector $y_{\mathrm{true}}$ and the predicted response vector $y_{\mathrm{pred}}$ are ranked across genes. Positive precision at 20, written as Pos P@20, measures how many of the 20 most up regulated genes in $y_{\mathrm{pred}}$ fall inside the top 100 most up regulated genes in $y_{\mathrm{true}}$. Negative precision at 20, written as Neg P@20, is defined in the same way for the 20 most down regulated genes. The final value is the mean across all test samples. These metrics do not replace MSE or PCC. They add a different view of prediction quality that is closer to practical use.

CAGNN gives the best Pos P@20 in the warm and cold drug splits. GSNN gives the best Pos P@20 in the cold cell split. For Neg P@20, CAGNN gives the best result in all three splits. Figure~\ref{fig:top_gene_placeholder} shows the same comparison in visual form for all three models.

\section{Correlation metrics}
The third group of metrics focuses on correlation. In this chapter, the main correlation metric is sample wise Pearson correlation coefficient. PCC measures whether the predicted response follows the correct gene level pattern within each sample. A higher value means better agreement in the shape of the response profile, even when the absolute size is not matched exactly.

\noindent Table~\ref{tab:pcc_results} shows the PCC results for the three splits. CAGNN gives the best PCC in the warm and cold drug settings. GSNN gives the best PCC in the cold cell setting and is the most stable model across the three splits. DeepCOP stays competitive in MSE in some settings, but its PCC drops more in the harder splits. This contrast between MSE and PCC is useful. A model can keep part of the gene ranking pattern while still making larger errors in absolute size.

\subsection{Regulation sign agreement}
\noindent A second metric can also be used to check gene level direction agreement. This metric asks whether the model predicts the sign of regulation correctly for each gene. For a given sample, the predicted response and the true response are compared gene by gene. A match means that both values are positive or both values are negative. In this calculation, no extra threshold is used.

\noindent For one sample with $G$ genes, the regulation sign accuracy is defined as
\[
\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{sign}}=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\mathbf{1}\left[\mathrm{sign}\!\left(\Delta^{\mathrm{pred}}_i\right)=\mathrm{sign}\!\left(\Delta^{\mathrm{true}}_i\right)\right].
\]
The final test score is the mean of this value across all test samples. This metric does not use the size of the response. It only checks whether the direction of change is correct.

\noindent Table~\ref{tab:sign_accuracy_results} shows the sign agreement results under the three splits. DeepCOP gives the highest sign accuracy in the warm split. CAGNN gives the highest sign accuracy in the cold drug split. GSNN gives the highest sign accuracy in the cold cell split. These results are consistent with the broader pattern seen in PCC. The cold cell setting is again the hardest setting for all models.

\subsection{Ablation analysis}
\noindent Correlation based results are also useful for ablation analysis because they show whether each component helps preserve the response pattern across genes. The comparison below focuses on how cell information is introduced into the graph model. Three variants are considered. The first variant uses only a cell identity embedding. The second replaces cell identity with a cell context derived from the control profile. The third keeps the same cell context and also injects it into the attention score as an extra context dependent term.

\noindent Table~\ref{tab:context_ablation} shows the corresponding test results. Replacing cell identity with a cell context gives a small PCC gain in the warm and cold drug settings, but the gain is much larger in the cold cell setting. This pattern suggests that a sample specific cell context is more useful than a fixed cell label when the model must generalize to unseen cell lines. Adding context aware attention on top of the cell context gives another improvement in the warm and cold drug settings. In the cold cell setting, the PCC gain is very small, but it stays positive. The MSE pattern is mixed. It improves slightly in warm and cold drug, while cold cell becomes slightly worse than the cell context only variant. This suggests that context aware attention mainly helps preserve the response pattern rather than uniformly reducing absolute error.

\noindent These results also clarify the different roles of context in the model. When context is added to the node features, it changes what each node representation is. It changes the sample specific state carried by each gene node before message passing starts. When context is added again inside attention, it changes how strongly nodes exchange information with their neighbors. The first affects node content. The second affects message passing. The gains in Table~\ref{tab:context_ablation} suggest that both roles are useful, but the larger benefit comes from replacing fixed cell identity with a cell context derived from the control profile.

\section{Uncertainty analysis}
The uncertainty aware version of the proposed model predicts both a mean response and a gene wise variance term. This makes it possible to study not only prediction accuracy, but also prediction reliability. The analyses in this section use the exported mean prediction $\mu$, the predicted variance $\sigma^{2}$, and the derived standard deviation $\sigma$. The aim is to examine whether the uncertainty output reflects real prediction difficulty and whether it helps explain the behaviour of the model across different evaluation settings.

\subsection{Uncertainty across splits}
\noindent A natural starting point is to compare the uncertainty distribution in the warm, cold drug, and cold cell settings. For each test sample, the gene wise uncertainty can be summarized by the average predicted standard deviation. These sample level values can then be compared across splits with a box plot or violin plot. This analysis is useful because the three splits represent different types of distribution shift. If the uncertainty head is meaningful, the harder settings should show a broader or higher uncertainty distribution. In particular, the cold cell split is expected to have the highest uncertainty because unseen cell lines introduce a stronger change in baseline state.

\subsection{Uncertainty and MSE}
\noindent The next analysis studies whether uncertainty is associated with absolute prediction error. For each test sample, the average predicted standard deviation can be plotted against the sample wise MSE. A positive relationship would show that the model tends to assign larger uncertainty to samples that are harder to predict in absolute value. This does not prove perfect calibration, but it does show whether the uncertainty output contains useful information about risk.

\subsection{Uncertainty and PCC}
\noindent A related analysis compares uncertainty with sample wise PCC. Here the same sample level uncertainty summary is compared with the PCC value of each sample. Since larger uncertainty should correspond to harder prediction, a negative relationship is expected. This view complements the MSE analysis. MSE focuses on absolute error size, while PCC focuses on whether the predicted response preserves the gene level pattern. Together, the two plots show whether uncertainty tracks both numerical error and loss of biological response pattern.

\subsection{Stratified performance by uncertainty}
\noindent The test samples can also be grouped by uncertainty level. A simple way is to divide them into low, medium, and high uncertainty groups using quantiles of sample level $\sigma$. The average MSE, PCC, top gene precision, and regulation sign accuracy can then be reported for each group. This analysis shows whether the uncertainty output can separate easier predictions from harder ones. If the grouping is informative, the low uncertainty group should have better predictive performance than the high uncertainty group. Figure~\ref{fig:uncertainty_summary_placeholder} shows one possible layout for these summary analyses.

\section{Performance with reduced training data}
This section studies how model performance changes when the amount of training data is reduced. The focus here is data efficiency. The aim is to see how much performance each model can retain when only a limited part of the training set is available.

\subsection{How the training set is reduced}
\noindent This analysis starts from a fixed warm split. The test set is kept unchanged for all budget levels. The training set is first built in the usual way as paired treatment control examples. Each paired example has a cell line label and a drug identity. A cell drug pair means all training examples that share the same cell line and the same drug.

\noindent The budget is then applied inside each cell drug pair. In the one shot setting, only one paired training example is kept for each cell drug pair. In the 20\%, 30\%, 50\%, and 80\% settings, the corresponding fraction of training examples is kept within each pair. For example, 20\% means that only 20\% of the available paired training examples are kept for that cell drug pair. The examples are sampled randomly within each pair. In the implementation, the kept sample count is rounded up, so each existing pair still contributes at least one example for any positive budget.

\noindent This design keeps the test set fixed and changes only the amount of training data. It also avoids letting large cell drug pairs dominate the whole analysis. Each model is trained under the same budget schedule and evaluated on the same fixed test set. The main metric in this analysis is sample wise PCC. PCC reflects whether the predicted response preserves the gene level pattern within each sample. MSE can also be reported, but the main goal is to track how quickly the response pattern emerges as training data increase.

\section{Discussion}
Taken together, the results in this chapter support a clear picture. Simple baselines can predict part of the perturbation response, but stronger generalization needs explicit modelling of drug information, cellular context, and biological structure. The graph based models are most useful when the test distribution moves away from the training data.

\noindent The results also show that a good warm split score should be read with care. Warm performance is important because it shows whether the model can fit the task. But it is not enough to claim strong biological generalization. The cold drug and cold cell settings are more useful. The cold cell result is especially important because it shows how hard it is to transfer perturbation knowledge across cellular contexts.

\noindent Several limitations remain. Drug target annotations are incomplete and may miss indirect or secondary mechanisms. The biological graph is curated, so it is biased toward well studied pathways. Some cell lines have much richer training support than others. The final benchmark is also limited to the 978 landmark genes. This keeps the task manageable, but it leaves much of the transcriptome outside the target. These points do not invalidate the approach, but they set clear limits on how the results should be read.

\noindent Another important lesson is methodological. Small details in data pairing and split construction can strongly change the apparent difficulty of the benchmark. The final pipeline in this thesis separates the split step from the control matching step and isolates the control pools of the training and test sets. This design reduces overly optimistic evaluation and makes the reported numbers more credible.

\section{Summary of findings}
Several findings stand out in this chapter. The benchmark comparison suggests that graph based models offer an advantage over simpler baselines, especially beyond the warm split. CAGNN combines graph attention, drug related features, and counterfactual regularization. The ablation analysis suggests that each of these parts contributes to the final result. The top gene analysis adds another view of model quality by showing whether the model can recover the strongest up regulated and down regulated genes.

\noindent The most difficult setting is generalization to unseen cell lines. This result shows both the value and the limit of the current framework. Prior molecular knowledge helps, but robust transfer across cellular contexts is still hard. This tension is central to the thesis. It leads into the next chapter, which discusses what was achieved, what remains hard, and which directions look most useful for future work.
